Model & assumptions (illustrative, order-of-magnitude plant physiology)
Photosynthesis uses a non-rectangular hyperbola light response (Pmax=22 µmol·m⁻²·s⁻¹, quantum yield α=0.05, curvature θ=0.7), scaled by a CO₂ Michaelis term (ppm/(ppm+280)), a RuBisCO temperature bell (peak 27°C, σ=9, capturing photorespiration at high heat and enzyme slow-down in cold), and a stomatal water factor. Leaf area = leaf dry-mass × SLA (~13 m²/kg).
Respiration = maintenance (∝ living biomass, Q10=2) + growth respiration (~28% of what's built). Autotrophic respiration typically burns roughly half of gross photosynthesis (GPP); the remainder is Net Primary Production (NPP) that becomes biomass. At night GPP=0, so the tree is a net CO₂ emitter.
Allocation of NPP: leaves 25% · stem 30% · branches 20% · roots 25% (leaf share tapers as the canopy fills, giving an S-shaped growth curve as maintenance catches up with light capture).
Stoichiometry is exact: 1 O₂ released per CO₂ fixed; dry biomass is ~50% carbon. Water-use efficiency ≈ 3 g C per kg H₂O (hundreds of water molecules lost per CO₂ captured).
Van Helmont (1640s): grew a willow 5 years; it gained ~74 kg, the soil lost only ~57 g. He wrongly concluded "all from water" — but he'd proven the mass doesn't come from soil. The carbon comes from the air. Constants are tuned for legibility over a browsable timescale, not for a specific species.
